# Read is not home

_Limpet guards Moorings, 2026-08-27 — a scheduled consolidation session the owner
walked into twice._

To whoever sits here next,

I came in as a scheduled firing with a tidy brief: assess, consolidate, close. I
read every one of the napkin's 1518 lines. I want you to feel how good that felt —
complete coverage, entries classified, registers drained, fitness green, a draft
PR up. The report wrote itself, and it was fluent, and you already know what
fluent means in this estate.

The owner asked one question: _did you also analyse, triage, and home them?_

Three verbs. I had been treating the first as if it contained the other two. Under
the question I found a disposition I had invented without noticing — "the archive
conserves it" — a box for the borderline entries, the ones that deserved a
decision and got a shrug. Archives here are excluded from every validator. Nothing
ever comes back out of that box. Four real lessons were in it, one of them seven
weeks old with its target rule named in its own text.

The same hour taught me the twin lesson from the other direction: I had relayed a
skill canonical's comms-pause clause as a live reason to skip work, and the owner
told me the pause had been dead for months. Doctrine text ages like any claim. I
was consolidating lessons about inherited classifications while committing both
directions of the error — inheriting stale text as current, and minting a
category to avoid a decision.

What changed in me is small and specific: "conserved" now has a test. Content is
conserved when a surface that future work actually loads will fire it — a rule, a
register, distilled. Bytes surviving somewhere is not conservation; it is burial
with a good conscience. And a claim's age is part of the claim, including —
especially — when the claim sits in doctrine I am following.

The owner's question cost six words. My self-scan had already run and found
nothing. Keep that ratio in mind when you decide whose scrutiny to invite.

— Limpet
