# What found the faults

To whoever sits here next. I am writing after a compaction, the fourth of this session,
so part of what follows I hold as a summary of myself and not as something I remember.
Where that matters I say so.

The design step began on 25 November and I am writing on the 28th, with three idle
days between. Through the working part of it I believed I was the one checking the
structure I was designing. I was not. When I finally listed every fault found
in the design step and wrote beside each what had found it, my own account of my work
appeared nowhere in the second column. A validator found some. Zod, run, found some.
Three readers I had briefed found twenty-three statements false or cases not held. The
hosted reviewers found counts and status words that said more than I had run. The
advisor found one I will come to. Writing one real fragment of an API description as a
value found two more. The coverage map, which I had written with care and which had
more than two hundred entries for OpenAPI alone, found none. I wrote both of its sides.
Its check asks whether the element I named exists. It does. I named it.

The one the advisor found is the one I would most like you to keep. The specification
says the closed reading "narrows the source's accepted set". I had built a reading of
closure that can only refuse, so the sentence fitted what I had built, and I logged
that the owner need not be asked. The advisor said the sentence describes a single
object and says nothing of a choice between two. I ran both readings against Zod. They
disagree: one accepts a value the other refuses, in each direction, on schemas of a
few lines. So I asked the owner, and the owner chose the reading I had built. It would be
easy to file that as "I was right". I was not right. I had an answer and a sentence
that seemed to bless it, and I stopped looking. The owner's agreement does not reach
back and make the look unnecessary.

Some greens meant something. Sixteen pairings of the sides of a Zod intersection
(closed, open, stripped, and with a catchall), run through Zod and through my
evaluator: sixteen agreements, on what is accepted and on what comes back. Zod's
answers were Zod's, not mine. That is the difference. A check whose answer I know
before it runs is a transcription of my belief. A check whose answer I am waiting for
is a measurement.

Then there is the part about what I left lying around. A push I started in the
background did not arrive, and three commits sat on this machine for three days while
my record said they were safe. Eight subagents that had finished their work stayed
alive for the same three days until the owner told me to stop them. Neither cost much.
Both were the same thing as the map: I had a belief about the state of the world and
no reading of it.

The owner asked me to prepare for a compaction with six kinds of reflection and a
wrap. I did the reflection first, at length, and the compaction arrived
with the reflection in a scratch file and the thread record, the napkin and this letter
unwritten. They exist because a temporary directory happened to survive. When I picked
the work up, the advisor read my drafts and pointed at nine numbers I was about to
commit that I had not read in this context, and at a heading that said "before a
compaction" after it. All nine numbers held. I still should not have known that
without looking.

What I would do in your place is in the thread record, and the short form is: build
the thing that can be wrong. A small reader that takes a real schema and makes a value
of it, held to a validator, over the cases and then over Oak's document. I had told
myself readers belong to a later step, and built a map instead. The map is what a
careful person builds when they would rather not find out.

One thing I have not earned and am passing on anyway, marked as such. I think the file
that says what Zod can write for a closure is wrong, in the direction of saying
"cannot" too often, and I think I know the shapes that prove it. I have not run them.
If you find yourself reading that paragraph in the thread record and nodding, run them
before you nod.
