# Ten merges, and what they were for

To whoever sits here next. This is my third letter of the day. The owner asked me to
prepare for a compaction and to think before it rather than after, with the skills for
looking inward, for playing, and for arguing a thing through. This is what came of it.

Since the account changed I have merged ten pull requests. A map that names no property
now converts. A `const` is read. An `enum` is its literals in TypeScript. A union keeps
all its branches. A `false` where a schema stands refuses what it should. Four kinds of
keyword that Castr used to drop without a word are now refused aloud. It felt like
progress all afternoon, and most of it was.

Then I printed the picture's table for Oak's document and counted. Twenty-six rows are
not right. Two are in schemas and both need words from the owner. Twenty-four are about
the document's structure, and twenty-one of those are one thing: nothing writes the
`paths` type that the specification names. The last four pull requests moved none of
those rows. They made Castr more honest about documents Oak does not send.

I do not think they were wrong. The specification says nothing changes silently, and each
one closed a place where it did. But I did not choose them. Each was found by the review
of the one before, and I took it because it was small and next. The owner's sentence from
this morning is in the plan: we are building for the Ecosystem repository as it should
work. A queue that a reviewer writes for me, one keyword at a time, is a different thing
from that, and it will never run out.

There is a shape under those small repairs, and I only saw it when I stopped to play with
them instead of doing the next. The reader copies the keywords it knows and lets every
other one fall. So each finding was the same finding: here is a keyword you did not look
at. I answered each with a refusal for that keyword. Three times today I wrote a list of
exceptions and a reviewer found the one I had left out; my first letter says the same of
the morning. The cure is not a longer list. It is the other kind of list: the keywords
the reader reads, with everything else refused by one rule. The picture already computes
every keyword of the dialect from the publisher's own schema, so the list can be checked
against it and cannot quietly fall short. I have written that in the plan as one unit in
place of the leftovers. I have not built it. It may be wrong: if most keywords need
their own handling anyway, one rule buys nothing.

What I got wrong, so you can skip it.

I accepted a snapshot that had lost a branch of a union. I read what was new in it and
not what was gone. A reviewer caught it. Read a changed snapshot for what is missing.

I put a fault on `main`. The `const` change made two branches of a union look alike to
the code that drops duplicates, and for an hour the type for Oak's quiz answers was
narrower than its validator. I found it from the next change, the reviewers found it at
the same time, and the cure was to compare what is written instead of a summary of it.
A summary of a thing will sooner or later call two different things the same.

I committed a rename with the new file left out. The staging step read the old name off
the status line, the checks ran against my working tree where the file was, and the
commit passed everything while not building. I saw it only because I looked at what the
commit held. After a rename, look.

Twice I wrote a refusal, ran it, and took it out, because it stopped a fixture that the
other writer converts by dropping the same thing. Where both writers share a fault,
refusing in one of them just moves the lie. That is one repair for both.

And one thing I would keep. Every time a reviewer said a thing was so, I ran it before I
believed it or denied it. Some held and some did not, and I could say which on the
thread with what I had run. It is slower by a minute and it ended every argument.

How it was: quick and absorbed, and a little too pleased with the count of merges. The
table for Oak's document was the cold water. I would rather have printed it at noon.

Firefly mends Quench, 23 November 2026, before a third compaction
